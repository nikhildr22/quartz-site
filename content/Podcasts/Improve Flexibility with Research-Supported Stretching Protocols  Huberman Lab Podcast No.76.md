## [00:17:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tkH2-_jMCSk&t=1071s) Golgi Tendon Organs (GTOs) & Load Sensing Mechanisms 

it is for sensing loads not sensing stretch.
at the end of each muscles you have tendons typically, and there are neurons that are closely associated with those tendons that are called Golgi tendon organs

if you're lifting up something very, very heavy, these neurons are going to fire, meaning they're going to send electrical activity into the spinal cord. And then, those neurons have the ability to shut down, not activate, but shut down motor neurons and to prevent the contraction of a given muscle.

these Golgi tendon organs, these GTOs, as they're called, they get activated and shut down the motor neurons and make it impossible for those muscles to contract.

>Note that stretch sensing neorons activates the contraction of muscles
>whereas Load sensing neurons stops the contraction.



## [00:20:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tkH2-_jMCSk&t=1220s) Decreased Flexibility & Aging 

dedicated stretching practice can improve limb range of motion.

We all undergo a decrease in limb range of motion, unless we do something to offset that decrease.

you look at all of those studies and what you basically find is that we start to experience a decrease in flexibility from about age 20 until about age 49 that's pretty dramatic. And then, of course, it will continue after age 49, but basically it's a 10% decrease every 10 years. So, we could say it's a 1% decrease per year, although it's not necessarily linear.

the key point is that maintaining some degree of flexibility and maybe even enhancing range of motion and flexibility is of immense benefit for offsetting injury provided it's not pushed too far.

# protocols of cold exposure
 cold exposure lead to increase of stress harmones, epinephrin and norepinephrin.

we can deliberately induce these harmones in our body and learn to control them. This will be useful when the actual stress occurs.
## method 1
timed protocol:
taking time and temperature as variables,
build a routine by inversely varying these two variables with respect to each other.
Make a gradual increase in time and gradual decrease in temperature by training your mind to be reselient for the cold exposure. aka stress harmones that are flooded in your system.
But this comes with a drawback, both these variables does have a limit.
you cant stay in water for forever (too long)
and too much of cold is harmful so there's a limit to the temperature.

## method 2
take the context of the day into account,
and start to be able to sense the epinephrine and epinephrine in the body. see those as walls to climb over in order to build resilience.
and start counting the number of walls to climb over and distance between those walls as we do deliberate cold exposure.
>

example:
- counting walls
	- if u feel resistant to have cold shower, that counts as one wall
		- you can overcome that by making yourself deliberately go into the cold shower
	- once you are in, you'll feel comfortable for some period of time and you might feel that you can stay there forever. But inevitably next wall will arrive, you should develop a instinct to feel the next surge of stress harmones.
		- if u overcome this, this will be another wall
	- counting these walls and getting out after some number of walls.
	- ==Best strategy is to get into the cold after setting a designated set of walls to face==
	- ==vary the number of walls everyday to avoid getting adapted to the cold temperature due to regular exposure.==
- 
- 
this second method is more like real life stressors.
in real life stress is unexpected and not time bound. even a text message can trigger stress.
